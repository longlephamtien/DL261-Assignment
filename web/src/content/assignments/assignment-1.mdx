## Problem statement

Single-label image classification, one prediction per image. The input is a grayscale image
$x \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 28 \times 28}$, the output is one of ten garment classes. Each model
returns raw logits $z \in \mathbb{R}^{10}$ and predicts $\hat{y} = \arg\max_i z_i$.

The aim is a controlled comparison of architecture families on one split under one protocol,
so that differences trace to the architecture rather than to the data. Macro-F1 is the
primary metric because it weights every class equally.

## Dataset and EDA

Fashion-MNIST: 70,000 grayscale images of $28 \times 28$ pixels, one channel, `uint8` in
$[0, 255]$, ten classes. The official 60,000 training images are split once, stratified by
class, with seed 0. Those indices are fixed and reused by every model; the official test set
is held out.

:::table{label="tab:splits"}
| Subset | Images | Per class |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| Train | 54,000 | 5,400 |
| Validation | 6,000 | 600 |
| Test | 10,000 | 1,000 |

Split sizes. The max-to-min per-class ratio is 1.000 in every subset, so the classes are
balanced and accuracy and macro-F1 are not distorted by class priors.
:::

Half of all training pixels are exactly zero and the garment is centred, so most of the 784
flattened features carry no signal. :ref[fig:samples] shows the variation within each class:
footwear and bags are distinctive, while the upper-body garments share a silhouette.

:::figure{label="fig:samples"}
![A grid of eight training examples for each of the ten Fashion-MNIST classes, one class per row](/DL261-Assignment/assignments/assignment-1/sample_grid.png)

Eight training examples per class.
:::

Class overlap is measurable before any training. Cosine similarity between class mean images
exceeds $0.98$ for Coat/Shirt, Pullover/Coat and Pullover/Shirt. A nearest-class-mean
classifier on raw pixels reaches $68.4\%$ validation accuracy, with recall $0.220$ for Shirt
against $0.872$ for Ankle boot: a floor the trained models must clear, and a prediction of
where they will fail. :ref[fig:overlap] shows both measurements.

:::figure{label="fig:overlap"}
![Two heatmaps: on the left, cosine similarity between the ten class mean images; on the right, the confusion matrix of a nearest-class-mean classifier on the validation split](/DL261-Assignment/assignments/assignment-1/class_similarity.svg)

Class mean similarity and the nearest-class-mean confusion matrix. Both isolate the same
upper-body garment cluster.
:::

The flattened view is highly redundant: ten principal components explain $72.1\%$ of pixel
variance and one hundred explain $91.4\%$. Pixel correlation falls from $0.747$ at a distance
of one pixel to $0.159$ at ten and $0.093$ at twenty, the local structure a convolution
exploits and a flattened view discards.

The same image also supports three sequence views, which the recurrent and Transformer
models will consume. Rows and columns give 28 timesteps of 28 features; $4 \times 4$ patches
give 49 tokens of 16. :ref[fig:sequences] shows how unevenly each view spreads its signal.

:::figure{label="fig:sequences"}

## Methodology

:::figure{label="fig:pipeline"}
![Pipeline from the dataset through the fixed split and preprocessing to the training loop and a run record](/DL261-Assignment/assignments/assignment-1/pipeline.svg)

Overview of the pipeline.
:::

:ref[fig:pipeline] shows the path from the dataset to a trained checkpoint. Normalization statistics are computed on the training split alone and recorded with each run. Augmentation, a random crop with edge padding of 2 pixels followed by a horizontal flip, is applied to training data only, so validation stays deterministic.

### Linear Classifier (Baseline)

The simplest baseline maps the flattened input image vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^{784}$ directly to output class logits $z \in \mathbb{R}^{C}$ through a single affine transformation:

$$
z = W x + b
$$

where $W \in \mathbb{R}^{10 \times 784}$ is the weight matrix and $b \in \mathbb{R}^{10}$ is the bias vector, yielding $7,850$ trainable parameters. Raw logits are passed directly to the Cross-Entropy loss without an explicit Softmax layer.

### Multilayer Perceptron (MLP)

To capture non-linear relationships and interactions among pixels, we implement a fully connected network with two hidden layers:

$$
\begin{aligned}
h_1 &= \text{Dropout}_{0.2}(\text{ReLU}(W_1 x + b_1)) \\
h_2 &= \text{Dropout}_{0.2}(\text{ReLU}(W_2 h_1 + b_2)) \\
z &= W_3 h_2 + b_3
\end{aligned}
$$

- **Hidden dimensions:** $784 \to 512 \to 256 \to 10$.
- **Activation function:** $\text{ReLU}(a) = \max(0, a)$ provides non-saturation and gradient propagation efficiency.
- **Regularization:** $\text{Dropout}(p=0.2)$ is applied after each hidden layer during training to reduce co-adaptation between units.
- **Complexity:** The architecture totals $535,818$ trainable parameters.

*(Note: Convolutional, Recurrent, and Transformer architectures will be integrated in Milestone 2).*

## Experimental setup

All models are trained under a strictly controlled experimental protocol:
- **Dataset:** Fashion-MNIST (10 classes, $28 \times 28$ grayscale images), using the committed split of :ref[tab:splits] ($54,000$ train, $6,000$ val, $10,000$ test) with seed $0$.
- **Preprocessing:** Normalization with training-split statistics, mean $0.2858$ and standard deviation $0.3529$; random crop and horizontal flip on the training loader only.
- **Optimization:** AdamW optimizer with initial learning rate $\eta = 3 \times 10^{-4}$, weight decay $\lambda = 0.05$, and batch size $128$.
- **Scheduler:** Cosine annealing learning rate schedule over $30$ epochs.
- **Model selection:** Checkpointing based on the highest validation Macro F1 score (`val_macro_f1`) with early stopping patience of $5$ epochs. The test split is not evaluated in this milestone.
- **Hardware:** Apple M4 Pro, 14 cores, 48 GB unified memory, MPS backend.
- **Software:** Python 3.12.0, PyTorch 2.14.0, torchvision 0.29.0, scikit-learn 1.9.1.

Accuracy and Macro F1 depend only on the seed and the fixed split, so they reproduce anywhere; times do not, so both models were trained back to back on an idle machine.

## Comparison and discussion

As demonstrated in :ref[tab:m1_preliminary_results], the Multilayer Perceptron outperforms the linear baseline, achieving an increase of **+11.61% in Macro F1** ($88.59\%$ vs $76.98\%$) for $68$ times the parameters and $1.09$ times the cost per epoch.

1. **Inductive Bias and Non-linearity:** The linear classifier is constrained to hyperplanar decision boundaries in pixel space $\mathbb{R}^{784}$, which is insufficient for resolving complex intra-class variations in clothing shapes (e.g., distinguishing Pullover vs Coat). The MLP, equipped with hidden layers and ReLU non-linearities, learns hierarchical representations capable of isolating non-linearly separable manifolds. The nearest-class-mean floor of $68.4\%$ and the linear model's $76.98\%$ sit closer to each other than either does to the MLP, so the linear model extracts little beyond class-mean templates.
2. **Training Dynamics:** The linear model saturates early and triggers early stopping at epoch 27 (best epoch 22), reflecting its limited capacity. In contrast, the MLP exhibits steady convergence across all 30 epochs without overfitting, aided by dropout regularization ($p=0.2$) and weight decay.
3. **Validation above Training:** In :ref[fig:curves] the validation scores exceed the training scores for both models. This is not leakage: augmentation and dropout are active during training only, so the training metrics are measured on a harder version of the task.
4. **Cost:** Parameter count is a poor proxy for cost at this scale, since both models are dominated by data loading rather than by their matrix multiplications. Inference latency will separate the architectures more sharply.
