## Problem statement

Single-label image classification, one prediction per image. The input is a grayscale image
$x \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 28 \times 28}$, the output is one of ten garment classes. Each model
returns raw logits $z \in \mathbb{R}^{10}$ and predicts $\hat{y} = \arg\max_i z_i$.

The aim is a controlled comparison of architecture families on one split under one protocol,
so that differences trace to the architecture rather than to the data. Macro-F1 is the
primary metric because it weights every class equally.

## Dataset and EDA

Fashion-MNIST: 70,000 grayscale images of $28 \times 28$ pixels, one channel, `uint8` in
$[0, 255]$, ten classes. The official 60,000 training images are split once, stratified by
class, with seed 0. Those indices are fixed and reused by every model; the official test set
is held out.

:::table{label="tab:splits"}
| Subset | Images | Per class |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| Train | 54,000 | 5,400 |
| Validation | 6,000 | 600 |
| Test | 10,000 | 1,000 |

Split sizes. The max-to-min per-class ratio is 1.000 in every subset, so the classes are
balanced and accuracy and macro-F1 are not distorted by class priors.
:::

Half of all training pixels are exactly zero and the garment is centred, so most of the 784
flattened features carry no signal. :ref[fig:samples] shows the variation within each class:
footwear and bags are distinctive, while the upper-body garments share a silhouette.

:::figure{label="fig:samples"}
![A grid of eight training examples for each of the ten Fashion-MNIST classes, one class per row](/DL261-Assignment/assignments/assignment-1/sample_grid.png)

Eight training examples per class.
:::

Class overlap is measurable before any training. Cosine similarity between class mean images
exceeds $0.98$ for Coat/Shirt, Pullover/Coat and Pullover/Shirt. A nearest-class-mean
classifier on raw pixels reaches $68.4\%$ validation accuracy, with recall $0.220$ for Shirt
against $0.872$ for Ankle boot: a floor the trained models must clear, and a prediction of
where they will fail. :ref[fig:overlap] shows both measurements.

:::figure{label="fig:overlap"}
![Two heatmaps: on the left, cosine similarity between the ten class mean images; on the right, the confusion matrix of a nearest-class-mean classifier on the validation split](/DL261-Assignment/assignments/assignment-1/class_similarity.svg)

Class mean similarity and the nearest-class-mean confusion matrix. Both isolate the same
upper-body garment cluster.
:::

The flattened view is highly redundant: ten principal components explain $72.1\%$ of pixel
variance and one hundred explain $91.4\%$. Pixel correlation falls from $0.747$ at a distance
of one pixel to $0.159$ at ten and $0.093$ at twenty, the local structure a convolution
exploits and a flattened view discards.

The same image also supports three sequence views, which the recurrent and Transformer
models will consume. Rows and columns give 28 timesteps of 28 features; $4 \times 4$ patches
give 49 tokens of 16. :ref[fig:sequences] shows how unevenly each view spreads its signal.

:::figure{label="fig:sequences"}
![On the left, standard deviation per timestep for row and column sequences, both rising from the image border towards the centre; on the right, a 7 by 7 heatmap of standard deviation per 4 by 4 patch token](/DL261-Assignment/assignments/assignment-1/sequence_profiles.svg)

Signal per timestep. No row falls below a quarter of peak signal, against one column and
three patch tokens, so patch tokenisation isolates background into a few weak tokens while
row and column sequences spread it across every step.
:::

## Methodology

:::figure{label="fig:pipeline"}
![Pipeline from the dataset through the fixed split and preprocessing to the training loop and a run record](/DL261-Assignment/assignments/assignment-1/pipeline.svg)

Overview of the pipeline.
:::

:ref[fig:pipeline] shows the path from the dataset to a trained checkpoint. Normalization
statistics are computed on the training split alone and recorded with each run. Augmentation, a random crop with edge padding of 2 pixels followed by a horizontal
flip, is applied to training data only, so validation stays deterministic.

**Linear classifier.** The image is flattened and mapped to logits by one affine layer:

$$
z = W x + b
$$

with $W \in \mathbb{R}^{10 \times 784}$ and $b \in \mathbb{R}^{10}$, $7{,}850$ parameters. The logits go directly to cross-entropy loss; no softmax is applied first.

**Multilayer perceptron.** Two hidden layers, $784 \to 512 \to 256 \to 10$, $535{,}818$ parameters:

$$
\begin{aligned}
h_1 &= \text{Dropout}_{0.2}(\text{ReLU}(W_1 x + b_1)) \\
h_2 &= \text{Dropout}_{0.2}(\text{ReLU}(W_2 h_1 + b_2)) \\
z &= W_3 h_2 + b_3
\end{aligned}
$$

ReLU does not saturate for positive inputs, which keeps gradients from vanishing across the hidden layers. Dropout at $p = 0.2$ after each hidden layer, with weight decay $0.05$, is the only regularization; both target co-adaptation in a model holding $68$ times the capacity of the linear baseline.

## Experimental setup

- **Data:** the split of :ref[tab:splits], seed 0.
- **Preprocessing:** normalization with training-split statistics, mean $0.2858$, standard deviation $0.3529$.
- **Optimization:** AdamW, $\eta = 3 \times 10^{-4}$, weight decay $0.05$, batch size 128.
- **Schedule:** cosine annealing over 30 epochs, early stopping after 5 epochs without improvement.
- **Checkpoint rule:** highest validation macro-F1. The test split is not evaluated in this milestone.
- **Hardware:** Apple M4 Pro, 14 cores, 48 GB unified memory, MPS backend.
- **Software:** Python 3.12.0, PyTorch 2.14.0, torchvision 0.29.0, scikit-learn 1.9.1.

Accuracy and macro-F1 depend only on the seed and the fixed split, so they reproduce anywhere; times do not, so both models were trained back to back on an idle machine.

## Comparison and discussion

In :ref[tab:baselines] the MLP gains $11.61$ macro-F1 points over the linear baseline, for $68$ times the parameters and $1.09$ times the cost per epoch.

**Capacity is the binding constraint.** The nearest-class-mean floor of $68.4\%$ and the linear model's $76.98\%$ sit closer to each other than either does to the MLP, so the linear model extracts little beyond class-mean templates. Hidden layers are what separate the Shirt, Coat and Pullover cluster.

**Validation scores exceed training scores for both models** in :ref[fig:curves]. This is not leakage: augmentation and dropout are active during training only, so training metrics are measured on a harder version of the task. Neither model overfits, and the MLP is still improving when the epoch budget ends.

**Parameter count is a poor proxy for cost at this scale.** Both models are dominated by data loading rather than by their matrix multiplications, which is why a $68\times$ larger model costs $9\%$ more per epoch. Inference latency will separate the architectures more sharply.
