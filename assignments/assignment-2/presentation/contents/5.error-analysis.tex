\vspace{-0.15cm}
\begin{block}{The error structure is a property of the task, not of the model}
Error rates differ by more than $2\times$ across the five configurations, yet the composition is
nearly identical. Improving the model lowers the total and leaves the proportions alone.
\end{block}

%----------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The Groups, and What Causes Them}
\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Thin structures, 24.7\%.} Pole $\rightarrow$ building is the single largest flow
          in the matrix (0.91M pixels, 5.9\% of all errors); pole $\rightarrow$ vegetation adds
          3.6\%. Pole is the hardest of the 19 classes (0.3230 IoU) \emph{despite appearing in 99\% of
          images}, so this is a \textbf{resolution failure, not data scarcity}. A stride-8
          feature map upsampled bilinearly cannot represent a structure a few pixels wide.
    \item \textbf{Upright surfaces, 24.0\%.} Building $\leftrightarrow$ vegetation, 1.16M and
          1.13M pixels, \emph{nearly symmetric}, which is the signature of a boundary error rather than a
          bias. Wall and fence add a taxonomy problem: low barriers separated from building by
          convention, not appearance.
    \item \textbf{Ground surfaces, 14.6\%.} The road/sidewalk kerb is a few pixels high and often
          occluded by parked cars. Grows to 20.3\% for the frozen arm.
    \item \textbf{Traffic participants, 6.5\%, and the one place a model differs in \emph{kind}.}
          12.5\% for the U-Net, nearly double. It scores \textbf{0.0205 on rider} against
          DeepLabV3's 0.4316. Rider and person differ only in what the person sits on, which
          requires recognising the vehicle beneath. \textbf{This is where pretrained features
          help most.}
\end{itemize}

\begin{block}{What would improve it}
Five of six groups are dominated by \emph{boundary} pixels $\Rightarrow$ the lever is
\textbf{resolution}, not the objective. Train and evaluate at native $1024 \times 2048$; add a
full-resolution decoder path. \textbf{Not} a reweighted loss: the ablation measures that
directly, and it makes things worse.
\end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{alertblock}{Refuted, and in the opposite direction}
Dice is worse on \emph{all nineteen} classes. Correlation between P(absent) and the damage Dice
does: $\mathbf{r = -0.809}$.
\end{alertblock}

%----------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Why Dice Fails Here}
\small
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
\begin{block}{The mechanism}
For a class with \emph{no} positive pixels in the batch:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cross-entropy} gives it no term of its own; it is only a competitor in the
          softmax denominator.
    \item \textbf{Dice} gives it a full term
          $\dfrac{0 + \varepsilon}{\sum_\Omega p_{cij} + 0 + \varepsilon}$,
          maximised by driving that class's probability to \textbf{zero everywhere}.
\end{itemize}
Dice \textbf{actively suppresses} every class missing from the current eight crops.
\end{block}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
\begin{block}{Measured}
\footnotesize
$10.43$ of 19 classes appear in a given $512^2$ crop; $15.92$ in a batch of 8.

So \textbf{16.2\%} of Dice terms in a typical step are suppression terms.

For \emph{train}, present in 2\% of crops: roughly \textbf{85\% of the 301 steps per epoch push
its logit down}, about 45 push it up.

$\Rightarrow$ the only exact zero in this report: under Dice the model never predicts a single
train pixel, on any seed, on either split.
\end{block}
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\vspace{-0.1cm}
\footnotesize
\textbf{It also explains the curve:} peaked at epoch 4 on all three seeds, train-to-validation gap
$-0.002$. Not overfitting, not underfitting: the suppression terms balance the overlap terms and
the objective stops producing useful gradient.

\textbf{What this does \emph{not} establish:} that class balancing is useless here. Accumulating
Dice over an epoch, or masking absent classes out of the mean, avoids this exact mechanism. Neither
was run.
\end{frame}
